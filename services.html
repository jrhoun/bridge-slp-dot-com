Specialized Areas of Treatment

Learn more about the specific areas we evaluate and treat.

Receptive language refers to how a child understands language (following directions, answering questions, understanding vocabulary). Expressive language refers to how a child uses words, gestures, and sentences to express thoughts, needs, and ideas. We help children expand their vocabulary, build grammatically correct sentences, and express themselves clearly.

Articulation refers to the physical production of specific speech sounds (e.g., /r/, /s/, /l/). Phonological disorders involve patterns of sound errors (e.g., dropping the ends of words, replacing back sounds with front sounds). We use evidence-based motor speech and phonological strategies to improve overall speech clarity and intelligibility.

Some children process language in whole chunks ("gestalts") or scripts from movies/TV rather than word-by-word. We specialize in NLA (Natural Language Acquisition) frameworks to support neurodivergent children and Gestalt language processors in transitioning from scripts to self-generated, flexible language.

Pragmatics involves the social use of language—such as turn-taking in conversation, understanding nonverbal cues, maintaining eye contact/engagement on their terms, and interacting naturally with peers and family members.
